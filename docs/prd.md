
I did a quick external check as part of this. There are a few important things that change the PRD.

A few strategic findings

1. The record itself is a strong acquisition engine.

Guinness currently lists the Tallest Cake record at 33 metres, achieved in Jakarta in 2008. Guinness also explicitly warns that records can change and that online information isn’t necessarily immediately updated.

That gives us a powerful content strategy around:

* Tallest cake in the world
* Tallest cake record
* Guinness World Records cake
* Nigeria tallest cake
* Nigeria Guinness World Record
* World record cake attempt
* Largest/tallest cake
* Charity cake
* Cake for charity
* Nigerian bakers world record
* African Guinness World Records
* Record-breaking cake

But we must not claim that 33m is still the live record on the event site without verifying the current Guinness status closer to the attempt.

2. Previous food records provide useful storytelling patterns.

For example, Guinness’ coverage of the Stratford University cake pyramid explicitly connected the record attempt to charity and required distribution of tens of thousands of slices as part of the food-record evidence.

That’s particularly relevant to National Charity Cake because “record + purpose + measurable public impact” is a much stronger story than simply “we want to make a giant cake.”

3. We shouldn’t rely on SEO alone.

SEO should bring people into the funnel, but the fundraising engine should be:

SEO + PR + influencers + corporate outreach + foundations + WhatsApp/social sharing + email + media + strategic partnerships + paid advertising

The website becomes the place where every traffic source eventually lands.

4. There are already Nigerian fundraising platforms we can study or potentially use as supplementary distribution channels.

For example, SooSeed positions itself as a Nigerian fundraising platform with campaign pages, donation tracking and local payment support.   ImpactHub similarly positions itself around fundraising and campaign management in Nigeria.

I wouldn’t make either one the core project infrastructure yet. But we should design the donation system so that an external fundraising campaign can coexist with the official site.

5. There are potential grant/foundation channels, but we shouldn’t put “apply to foundations” into the website as if they are guaranteed funding sources.

For example, the Nigeria Youth Futures Fund currently has 2026 funding programmes supporting youth ecosystem development and encourages projects involving technology, media, arts, entertainment and creativity.

The outreach strategy should therefore maintain a funding prospect database separately from the public website.

⸻

And yes, the website needs to answer, almost immediately:

“I want to help. What exactly can I do?”

A sponsor shouldn’t have to hunt.

A donor shouldn’t have to hunt.

A journalist shouldn’t have to hunt.

A volunteer shouldn’t have to hunt.

Someone who wants to donate ₦50m shouldn’t have to fill out a generic contact form.

1. Product Overview

The National Charity Cake website is the official digital home for a national humanitarian and record-attempt initiative led by Evanna Empowerment Initiative.

The initiative aims to pursue the Guinness World Records title for the tallest cake while creating opportunities for skills development, volunteer participation, corporate collaboration, fundraising and community impact.

The website must function as more than an informational website.

It must operate as the project’s:

* Public information hub
* Fundraising destination
* Partnership acquisition platform
* Volunteer recruitment channel
* Media resource centre
* Project transparency portal
* SEO/content engine
* Campaign conversion platform
* Official source of project information

The website should be designed to support the project before, during and after the November 2026 event.

⸻

2. Product Vision

Create the definitive digital destination for the National Charity Cake where anyone can understand the project within minutes and immediately know how they can contribute.

The website should make the following journey possible:

Discover → Understand → Trust → Choose how to help → Take action → Stay involved

64. Guinness World Records Content Rules

The website should maintain a dedicated internal checklist for record-related claims.

Before publishing any record statement:

* Verify current record information.
* Verify project application/status.
* Use approved record terminology.
* Avoid implying Guinness endorsement.
* Avoid claiming success before official confirmation.
* Preserve evidence and documentation.
* Update the website immediately when status changes.

The current public Guinness page identifies the tallest cake as 33m, achieved in Indonesia in 2008, but Guinness itself notes that records can change and online records may not immediately reflect the latest status. (⁠Guinness World Records)

75. Critical Product Decision

The website should be built around this principle:

The website informs. The campaign converts. The team closes.

The website should not attempt to replace the project team.

A ₦100 million potential partner needs a human conversation.

A foundation needs documentation.

A volunteer needs a form.

A ₦25,000 donor needs a frictionless payment flow.

A journalist needs verified information.

A casual visitor needs a compelling story.

The product should optimize each journey accordingly.
